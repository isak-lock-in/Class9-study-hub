1. What is a Polynomial?

A polynomial is an algebraic expression made up of variables, constants and powers of variables combined using addition, subtraction and multiplication.

📌 Definition:

An expression of the form

a₀ + a₁x + a₂x² + a₃x³ + ... + aₙxⁿ

is a polynomial in the variable x, where the coefficients are real numbers and the powers are non-negative integers.
Example:

3x² + 5x − 7

This is a polynomial in x.

2. Terms, Coefficients and Constants

A polynomial is made up of terms. Each term may contain a coefficient and a variable.

Consider:

5x³ − 2x² + 7x − 9

The terms are:
  • 5x³
  • −2x²
  • 7x
  • −9
The numerical factors 5, −2 and 7 are coefficients.

−9 is a constant term.

3. Degree of a Polynomial

The degree of a polynomial is the highest power of the variable having a non-zero coefficient.

For:

4x⁵ + 2x³ − 7x + 8

The highest power of x is 5.

Therefore: Degree = 5
⭐ Remember:

Look for the highest exponent of the variable.

4. Types of Polynomials

Polynomials can be classified according to the number of terms they contain.

Monomial

A polynomial having one term is called a monomial.

Examples:

5x, 7x², 9

Binomial

A polynomial having two unlike terms is called a binomial.

Examples:

x + 5
3x² − 7

Trinomial

A polynomial having three unlike terms is called a trinomial.

Examples:

x² + 3x + 2
2x³ − x + 5

5. Constant Polynomial

A polynomial containing only a constant term is called a constant polynomial.

Examples:

5, −3, 12, 0
⭐ A non-zero constant polynomial has degree 0.

6. Zero Polynomial

A polynomial in which all coefficients are zero is called the zero polynomial.

0
⭐ The degree of the zero polynomial is not defined.

7. Value of a Polynomial

To find the value of a polynomial for a particular value of the variable, substitute that value for the variable and simplify.

Let:

p(x) = x² + 3x + 2

Find p(2).

Substitute x = 2:

p(2) = 2² + 3(2) + 2

= 4 + 6 + 2

= 12

8. Zero of a Polynomial

A number a is called a zero of a polynomial p(x) if p(a) = 0.

p(a) = 0 → a is a zero of p(x)
Consider:

p(x) = x − 5

Put x = 5:

p(5) = 5 − 5 = 0

Therefore: 5 is a zero of p(x).

9. Operations on Polynomials

Polynomials can be added, subtracted and multiplied by using algebraic rules.

Addition

(3x + 5) + (2x + 7)

= 3x + 2x + 5 + 7

= 5x + 12

Subtraction

(5x + 8) − (2x + 3)

= 5x + 8 − 2x − 3

= 3x + 5

Multiplication

2x(x + 3)

= 2x² + 6x

10. Important Algebraic Identities

These identities are useful when expanding and factorising polynomial expressions.

(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
(a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²
(a + b)(a − b) = a² − b²
(x + a)(x + b) = x² + (a + b)x + ab
⭐ Learn these identities carefully. They are used repeatedly in polynomial simplification and factorisation.

11. Factorisation

Factorisation means expressing an algebraic expression as a product of its factors.

Consider:

x² + 5x + 6

We need two numbers whose product is 6 and whose sum is 5.

The numbers are 2 and 3.

Therefore:

x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)

12. Remainder Theorem

When a polynomial p(x) is divided by x − a, the remainder is p(a).

Remainder = p(a)
Let:

p(x) = x² + 3x + 2

When divided by x − 1:

Remainder = p(1)

= 1² + 3(1) + 2

= 6

13. Factor Theorem

If p(a) = 0, then x − a is a factor of p(x).

p(a) = 0 ⇔ (x − a) is a factor of p(x)
Let:

p(x) = x² − 5x + 6

Check x − 2:

p(2) = 4 − 10 + 6

= 0

Therefore: x − 2 is a factor.

🧠 Quick Recap

  • A polynomial contains variables with non-negative integer powers.
  • The highest power gives the degree.
  • One term → monomial.
  • Two terms → binomial.
  • Three terms → trinomial.
  • p(a) = 0 means a is a zero of p(x).
  • Remainder when dividing by x − a is p(a).
  • If p(a) = 0, x − a is a factor.
  • Algebraic identities help with expansion and factorisation.
