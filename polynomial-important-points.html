1. Definition of a Polynomial

A polynomial is an algebraic expression in which the powers of the variable are non-negative integers.

Examples:

x² + 3x + 2
5x³ − 2x + 7
9
⭐ Powers such as −1, 1/2, etc. do not belong to an ordinary polynomial in x.

2. Degree of a Polynomial

The degree is the highest power of the variable with a non-zero coefficient.

For:

7x⁴ + 3x² − 5x + 8

Degree = 4
⭐ Always look for the highest exponent.

3. Types by Number of Terms

1 term → Monomial

2 terms → Binomial

3 terms → Trinomial
Monomial: 5x²

Binomial: x + 7

Trinomial: x² + 3x + 2

4. Value of a Polynomial

To find p(a), substitute x = a into the polynomial.

If:

p(x) = x² + 2x + 1

Then:
p(2) = 2² + 2(2) + 1
= 4 + 4 + 1
= 9

5. Zero of a Polynomial

a is a zero of p(x) if:

p(a) = 0
p(x) = x − 4

p(4) = 4 − 4 = 0

Therefore: 4 is a zero of p(x).

6. Important Algebraic Identities

(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
(a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²
(a + b)(a − b) = a² − b²
(x + a)(x + b) = x² + (a + b)x + ab
🔥 These identities are extremely important for expansion and factorisation.

7. Factorisation

Factorisation means expressing a polynomial as a product of its factors.

x² + 5x + 6

Find two numbers whose:
Product = 6
Sum = 5

The numbers are 2 and 3.

Therefore: x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)

8. Remainder Theorem

When p(x) is divided by x − a, the remainder is p(a).

Remainder = p(a)
If:

p(x) = x² + 3x + 2

Divide by x − 1.

Remainder = p(1)
= 1² + 3(1) + 2
= 6

9. Factor Theorem

p(a) = 0 ⇔ (x − a) is a factor of p(x)
⭐ If you are asked whether x − a is a factor, substitute x = a into p(x).

If the result is 0, it is a factor.

10. Zero Polynomial

The polynomial containing only zero is called the zero polynomial.

p(x) = 0
⭐ The degree of the zero polynomial is not defined.

11. Constant Polynomial

A non-zero polynomial containing only a constant has degree 0.

Examples:

5
−8
12
⭐ Non-zero constant polynomial → Degree 0.
