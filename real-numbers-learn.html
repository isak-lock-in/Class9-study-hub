CHAPTER 2

Real Numbers

Learn the important concepts of Real Numbers step by step.

1. Number Systems

Real numbers include both rational and irrational numbers. Different number systems are related to each other.

Natural Numbers ⊂ Whole Numbers ⊂ Integers ⊂ Rational Numbers ⊂ Real Numbers

Natural Numbers

Counting numbers such as 1, 2, 3, 4, ...

Whole Numbers

Natural numbers together with 0.

Integers

Positive numbers, negative numbers and zero.

Example: -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3

Rational Numbers

A number that can be written in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

Rational number = p/q,   q ≠ 0

2. Decimal Form of Rational Numbers

A rational number can have a terminating decimal or a non-terminating recurring decimal representation.

Terminating Decimal

A decimal that ends after a finite number of digits.

1/2 = 0.5
3/4 = 0.75

Non-terminating Recurring Decimal

A decimal that continues forever but repeats a pattern.

1/3 = 0.3333...
2/11 = 0.181818...
💡 A useful test for a rational number in lowest form: if the denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and/or 5, its decimal representation terminates.

3. Irrational Numbers

An irrational number cannot be expressed as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

Examples: √2, √3, √5 and π

Their decimal expansions are non-terminating and non-recurring.

4. Real Numbers

Rational and irrational numbers together form the set of real numbers.

Real Numbers = Rational Numbers ∪ Irrational Numbers

Every point on the ordinary number line represents a real number.

5. Square Roots and Surds

A square root of a number is a value which, when multiplied by itself, gives the original number.

√25 = 5 because 5 × 5 = 25

A surd is an irrational root that cannot be simplified to a rational number.

√2 and √3 are examples of surds.
💡 Always simplify the square factors inside a surd before doing further calculations.

6. Simplifying Surds

Look for perfect-square factors inside the radical.

√12 = √(4 × 3)

= 2√3
√50 = √(25 × 2)

= 5√2

7. Operations on Quadratic Surds

Addition and Subtraction

Only like surds can be directly added or subtracted.

3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2

Multiplication

√2 × √3 = √6

Division

√6 / √2 = √3

8. Rationalization

Rationalization means transforming a denominator containing a surd into a rational number.

1/√2

Multiply numerator and denominator by √2:

√2/2
💡 For a denominator such as a + √b, its conjugate is a − √b.

9. Absolute Value

The absolute value of a number represents its distance from zero on the number line.

|5| = 5
|-5| = 5

Absolute value is never negative.

10. Quick Revision

  • Rational numbers can be written as p/q.
  • Irrational numbers cannot be written as p/q.
  • Rational + irrational numbers together form the real numbers.
  • Terminating decimals have a finite number of digits.
  • Recurring decimals repeat a pattern indefinitely.
  • Simplify surds before performing operations.
  • Only like surds can be directly added or subtracted.
  • Rationalization removes surds from denominators.
  • Absolute value represents distance from zero.
